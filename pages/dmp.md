# Data Management Plan

## Project Overview
This project is a collection of sunset and sunrise photographs. Focusing on photographic prints, this collection features a range of photographic techniques, including images produced from photochrom processes and lantern slides. The goal of this project is to capture the ways sunsets and sunrises have been depicted across time and mediums. Images are selected by looking at vibrancy, choosing pieces that catch the eye, and paying special attention to light contrasts in black and white photographs. While this collection focused primarily on photographs, the line between artistic renderings and traditional photographs are not all clear. Photographs produced in mediums such as photochrom prints toe this line toward a more illustrative look and are still represented in the collection.

*Tools and Access*

Tools and software needed to work with our data/ reproduce our project includes: Google Drive to store images, document ideas, create spreadsheets for our metadata. GitHub and collection builder are needed to create the site for the public to be able to look at our collection. GitHub was used to personalize our collection for users to navigate our collection with ease. You are able to access these tools via the internet, all you need is an email to be able to create accounts for Google Drive and GitHub.

## Storage and Stewardship

*During the Project*

Google Drive - This contained our images folder, metadata sheets, project description and metadata plan documents for group collaboration.
GitHub - This was the main repository. Only the creator had access to settings. Once added, group members can edit the code and commit changes.
Personal computers - This is where we stored image files. Contributors also had individual access to Google drive via personal emails.

*After Term*

Once the term ends, we don't plan to actively maintain the project. If GitHub Pages stops serving the site, we likely wouldn't migrate it elsewhere. The collection was a class project to help us practice data management for digital collections. The repository would remain public by default, but we wouldn't put effort into preserving or continuing it. Letting it quietly go offline is the plan. Archived mode feels like a natural ending for a project like this.

## Access, Reuse, and Rights

*Our License*

All of the images in this collection are either in the public domain or old enough that they are no longer protected by copyright, so anyone is free to download and use them however they like. The written descriptions, metadata, and other content our group created for the site are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). This basically means you can freely use, share, or remix anything we wrote as long as you give our team credit. We chose this license because we want the collection to be as open and accessible as possible! Whether you're a student, an artist, or just someone who loves sunsets, you should be able to use what you find here without jumping through hoops.

*Rights Implications of our Sources*

Since all of the images in this collection come from before 1930 or are explicitly in the public domain, the originating institutions such as museums, libraries, and archives do not hold copyright over the images themselves. However, some institutions may assert additional rights or restrictions over their digital reproductions of public domain works. Users should be aware of this and check the policies of the original source institution if they plan to use an image in a published or commercial context. That said, no fair use arguments are necessary for this collection since we are not reproducing copyrighted material, public domain works can be used freely without needing to invoke fair use. There are no objects in this collection with more restrictive copyright terms, though some images sourced from specific institutions may come with preferred citation practices or terms of use that we encourage users to respect out of courtesy, even when not legally required.

*Ethical Considerations*

While a sunset collection is ethically straightforward, there are a few things worth noting. Some landscapes may hold cultural or spiritual significance to Indigenous or local communities, and users should be mindful that certain locations are more than just scenic backdrops. Some older images, like lantern slides and photochrom prints, were also produced during a colonial era when landscapes were captured and distributed without input from the communities who lived there. Some locations also reflect historical place names. Users and researchers should keep that historical context in mind when engaging with these materials. On the curation side, we made a point to only include images with clear copyright status, which we consider an ethical responsibility to both users and original creators. While this collection does not raise major ethical red flags, we believe even nature photography deserves thoughtful consideration of the history and context behind how and why images were made.
