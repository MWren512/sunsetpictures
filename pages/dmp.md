# Data Management Plan

## Project Overview
This project is a collection of sunset and sunrise photographs. Focusing on photographic prints, this collection features a range of photographic techniques, including images produced from photochrom processes and lantern slides. The goal of this project is to capture the ways sunsets and sunrises have been depicted across time and mediums. Images are selected by looking at vibrancy, choosing pieces that catch the eye, and paying special attention to light contrasts in black and white photographs. While this collection focused primarily on photographs, the line between artistic renderings and traditional photographs are not all clear. Photographs produced in mediums such as photochrom prints toe this line toward a more illustrative look and are still represented in the collection.

Once the term ends, we don't plan to actively maintain the project. If GitHub Pages stops serving the site, we likely wouldn't migrate it elsewhere. The collection was a class project to help us practice data management for digital collections. The repository would remain public by default, but we wouldn't put effort into preserving or continuing it. Letting it quietly go offline is the plan. Archived mode feels like a natural ending for a project like this.

## Access, Reuse, and Rights

***Our License***

All of the images in this collection are either in the public domain or old enough that they are no longer protected by copyright, so anyone is free to download and use them however they like. The written descriptions, metadata, and other content our group created for the site are licensed under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 (CC BY 4.0). This basically means you can freely use, share, or remix anything we wrote as long as you give our team credit. We chose this license because we want the collection to be as open and accessible as possible! Whether you're a student, an artist, or just someone who loves sunsets, you should be able to use what you find here without jumping through hoops.

***Rights Implications of our Sources***

Since all of the images in this collection come from before 1930 or are explicitly in the public domain, the originating institutions such as museums, libraries, and archives do not hold copyright over the images themselves. However, some institutions may assert additional rights or restrictions over their digital reproductions of public domain works. Users should be aware of this and check the policies of the original source institution if they plan to use an image in a published or commercial context. That said, no fair use arguments are necessary for this collection since we are not reproducing copyrighted material, public domain works can be used freely without needing to invoke fair use. There are no objects in this collection with more restrictive copyright terms, though some images sourced from specific institutions may come with preferred citation practices or terms of use that we encourage users to respect out of courtesy, even when not legally required.

***Ethical Considerations***

While a sunset collection is ethically straightforward, there are a few things worth noting. Some landscapes may hold cultural or spiritual significance to Indigenous or local communities, and users should be mindful that certain locations are more than just scenic backdrops. Some older images, like lantern slides and photochrom prints, were also produced during a colonial era when landscapes were captured and distributed without input from the communities who lived there. Some locations also reflect historical place names. Users and researchers should keep that historical context in mind when engaging with these materials. On the curation side, we made a point to only include images with clear copyright status, which we consider an ethical responsibility to both users and original creators. While this collection does not raise major ethical red flags, we believe even nature photography deserves thoughtful consideration of the history and context behind how and why images were made.

## Data Dictionary

| Field Name | Definition | Example Value | Notes on Use |
| ------------------ | ----------------- | ------------------- | ------ |
| **objectid** | The unique identifier given to each object in the collection. | object1 | The objectid was simply this name and a corresponding number. Objects were assigned their unique identifier based on the order in which they were added to the metadata sheet. |
| **filename** | The unique name given to each photograph. | newyork_sunset.jpeg | The filename was created by including whether the object depicted a sunset or sunrise, followed by an identifying location (i.e., “sunset_arizona_highsmith.jpg”) and creator. Filenames were formatted according to GitHub and CollectionBuilder guidelines, including use of lowercase and “_” in place of spaces. Where a creator or location was not easily known or too long to be an effective filename, a describing feature was just used (i.e., “library_at_sunset”). |
| **format** | Indicates whether the image file is in JPG/JPEG or PNG format. | image/jpeg | Both JPG and JPEG files are listed as image/jpeg. PNG files are just listed as image/png. Any other files (i.e., webp) were converted to JPG/JPEG file. |
| **title** | The unique title given to each object in the collection. | Sunrise at Sea | The titles for the images were primarily drawn from each object’s associated title on the source catalog record. Each title indicates whether the image is of a sunset or sunrise. It also provides a little description of the image being depicted - this typically includes the location being represented (i.e., Sunset from Pike’s Peak). For images whose title was transliterated, the approach remains the same. In this particular case, the title of the image selected roughly translated into “Sunrise over the Sea”, which fits the above criteria and was thus used. For images where the title was lacking these identifiers (e.g., “library” to “Library at Sunset”) or a series of numerals rather than a descriptive title, this information was added or adjusted the title. | 
| **date** | The year the photograph or print was created, taken from the source catalog record. | 1919 | When a date range was given, we used the middle year as an approximate date, including the prefix “c.” (e.g., 1900-1906 would be listed as c. 1903). If an estimated date from the source catalog record is already listed as “circa”, this is changed to “c.”. | 
| **source** | The URL link to the source catalog where the associated photograph was taken. | https://flic.kr/p/nXYPQk | The URL link was copied directly from the browser computer tab for the associated image. |
| **rightsstatement** | The rights and access statement associated with the image, taken from the source catalog record. | http://rightsstatements.org/vocab/NoC-US/1.0/ | The URLs for the rights statements were copied from https://rightsstatements.org/en/, often linked from the source catalog record. In cases where access and use was noted as “open” or “unrestricted” and the source catalog was a U.S. institution, No Known Copyright was used for the rightsstatement. All images from international institutions had specific creative commons licenses used, and therefore the link listed was copied. |
| **creator** | The name of the photographer, company, or associated creator, taken from the source catalog record. | Highsmith, Carol M. | Where a specific creator is unknown, the field is written as "unknown". Names of people are written with last name first, followed by first name (e.g., Carol M. Highsmith is recorded as Highsmith, Carol M.). This is separated by a comma. Names of organizations, companies, and usernames are written as they appear on the source catalog record (e.g., Detroit Photographic Co.). | 
| **subject** | The subjects depicted with the associated object. Labels for the different mediums are their own separate field in this collection. | Sunrise–Mountains | The subject labels were created following the format [category]--[subject]. If subjects are not discernable in the image, the object just receives a category tag. Multiple subject labels are separated by a semicolon (;). The images were sorted into two primary categories - sunrises and sunsets. The subjects were generated based off of the main images represented in the collection materials (i.e., “water”, “mountains”, and “clouds” were prominent and frequent features). This was inspired by subject tags found on various source catalog records, but our subjects are not taken directly from any one particular source catalog. |
| **hue** | The primary color hue of the associated image; categorized as warm, cool, or black & white. | Warm | The hue of an image is based off a traditional color wheel. Images with primarily blue, green, and purple tones were labeled “cool” while images with more prominent red, orange, and yellow colors were labeled “warm”. Since the collection included non-color objects as well, any images that were not in color were listed as Black & White. |
| **medium** | The method or technique used to make the photograph, taken from the source catalog record. | Photochrom print | The medium was tagged to showcase the various techniques and photographic processes represented in the collection. Objects listed as “image”, “still image”, or with no associated medium, are listed as “photograph”. | 
| **location** | The location the photograph was captured, taken from the source catalog record. | Page, Arizona | For locations in the United States, locations are formatted as [city], [state] when these locations are known. Regional locations are written as is, capitalized (e.g., Eastern Colorado). For locations outside the United States, the location is copied from the source catalog record when available. If a country is not included already, we added it at the end (e.g., “Cathedral Peaks, Lake Manawapouri” became “Lake Manawapouri, New Zealand”). In the case that a U.S. state or Country are the only identifier of location (e.g., Thailand), then the latitudinal and longitudinal coordinates of the corresponding capital are included (i.e., Bangkok for Thailand). Unknown locations are left blank. |
| **latitude** | The latitudinal coordinates for the location the photograph was taken. | 38.7467 | Coordinates were gathered based on the information given in the “location” field. These were initially accessed via Wikipedia, clicking on the coordinates listed in degrees format, which then directed to GeoHack. The latitude decimal coordinate was listed in the metadata first for ease of copying coordinates in the correct order. |
| **longitude** | The longitudinal coordinates for the location the photograph was taken. | -105.1783 | Coordinates were gathered based on the information given in the “location” field. These were initially accessed via Wikipedia, clicking on the coordinates listed in degrees format, which then directed to GeoHack. The longitudinal decimal coordinate was listed in the metadata second for ease of copying coordinates in the correct order. |
